    How to read this

    • Required drawing (left of each card) is drawn from the order schedule: rough-opening size and operation. Quantities above 1 show the count.
    • Pictures are each bidder's own drawing for that line, taken from their quote. NF's quote has no drawings, so its tiles show the catalog photo of the quoted product series; Prima's and Yuegangwan's quotes use stock product photos, not unit drawings. Click any picture to enlarge.
    • Match means quantity, size (within ½ in) and operation agree with the order. Color is not scored — bidders were not given one finish schedule.
    • Bars in each card compare extended price (unit × quantity) within that opening only.
    • Landed estimates for imports add 50–75% U.S. import duty on full value (Section 232 aluminum, plus possible Section 301), freight, and 7.75% Utah sales/use tax. Andersen adds tax only. Vista's installed price has installation and tear-out removed so it compares as goods. Installation is otherwise excluded.
    • U-factor (heat loss; lower is better) is shown as each bidder states it. Code limit for windows and doors here is U ≤ 0.32 (Utah Code 15A-3-701 amends the IECC climate-zone-5 value of 0.30; the permit set AE601 also specifies 0.32).     What the house needs: placement and equipment sizes

Each indoor unit has its own line pair straight from its outdoor unit, so there are no branch boxes and no ducts. Wall-mounted units are excluded (owner rule). The plans follow the architect's sheet orientation (AE101/AE102, 10.16.2026) and are schematic.

    • *Share = the indoor unit's fraction of its outdoor unit's 5 °F capacity after the 4% altitude derate (28,800 Btu/h for a 42–45K ODU-M; 24,960 for the 36K ODU-L), split by nominal size. It is a worst case for when every unit calls at once. In practice the inverter sends spare capacity to whichever rooms call: ODU-M has ~28,800 against a 19,500 load.
    • ⚠ Cassette mounting height: the Bestcold cassette nameplate (the same OEM hardware as TCL) says to mount with the lowest moving parts at least 8.2 ft (2.5 m) above the floor. At a standard 8 ft ceiling a flush cassette sits at about 7.9 ft. Measure the ceiling height in every cassette room and get written confirmation from the maker before ordering. The A2L minimum-room-area check also depends on this height.
    • Cassette fit: lower-level bays are 2×10 at 16 in. o.c. (14½ in. clear, 9¼ in. deep). A slim one-way cassette (≤ 14¼ × 8¾ in. body) fits between joists. The 4-way compact cassettes offered so far (22½ in. square, 10¼ in. tall) need one joist headered off plus ¾ in. furring at each location.
    • Why two great-room consoles at 12k + 9k: one unit at each end of the glass counters downdraft along its full length, and keeping them ≤ 12k leaves room on the combination chart for M-5. The great-room load is 9,500 Btu/h (≤ 10,600); the two get 14,400 at 5 °F (base).
    • No floor console under 18k (TCL confirmed 11/04; Bestcold is the same OEM hardware). The 12k + 9k consoles above are the target, but these lines offer only the 18k floor/ceiling unit (41½ × 26⅝ × 9¼ in.). Two workable great-room layouts:
      • Option A, two floor units: M-1 and M-2 both 18k floor/ceiling (9+12+18+18; on chart; 136% connected). Simple, but two bulky units, and M-5 cannot be added.
      • Option B, one floor unit plus a floor-supply slim duct (preferred if the structure allows):
        • M-1 is an 18k floor unit at the glass end.
        • M-2 is a 9k slim duct (7⅞ in. tall) concealed in the floor framing below the kitchen end, with a linear floor grille along the glass. A perimeter floor supply under glazing is the classic downdraft fix.
        • Combinations: 9+9+12+18 (114%), or 9+9+9+12+18 with M-5. Both are on chart.
        • Costs: one joist header and a short plenum in the Bed 009 ceiling, a return grille in the great-room floor, and a mixed AHRI rating (TCL publishes one).
    • Why the optional unit goes in the entry:
      • Entry: it has the largest load with no unit of its own (1,530 Btu/h at 14 °F), takes the front-door infiltration, and the open stair to the lower level drives stack-effect drafts. It sits beside the riser chase, so its line set is the shortest on ODU-M.
      • Closet (1,950 Btu/h, unoccupied): better served by a door undercut or transfer grille from M-3, plus air sealing of its exterior walls.
      • Ensuite (890 Btu/h): better served by an electric radiant mat under the tile (comfort where you stand barefoot; no cassette in a humid room).
      • Mudroom and powder (480 and 60 Btu/h): too small to justify a unit.
    • Loads are medians from the project load model (Manual J-style estimate, not a permit Manual J). Intervals reflect envelope-input uncertainty only. Line routes and joist direction are schematic; verify them on site.

    How to read this

    • Requirements come from the ductless heat pump requirements document (two-outdoor-unit path, §7) and the owner rule of 11/01/2026: cassettes and floor units only.
    • Status: meets meets with a caveat fails not documented, so ask not applicable. “Not documented” is not a pass; it is an open item on the request for information.
    • Capacities are nameplate values from each bidder's sheet; “derated” applies the ~4% altitude loss at 4,900 ft. Low-temperature figures from overseas spec sheets are manufacturer claims until backed by an AHRI or NEEP listing.
    • Same hardware, three labels: the Bestcold, ZERO and TCL outdoor units share cabinet size, charge, line-set limits and capacity tables. Compare them on indoor-unit offering, documentation, warranty and price.
    • Prices: only the two local contractors have quoted, and neither quoted a ductless heat pump. Overseas prices will need duty, freight, sales tax and a separate installer added before they can be compared.
